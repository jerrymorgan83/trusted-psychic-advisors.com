Overview and history

Keen is one of the longer-established names in online and phone-based advice and readings. Its marketplace includes psychics, tarot readers, astrologers, mediums and other advisors, each setting their own rate and listing their own specialties. A company press release in 2025 described the platform’s emphasis on trust and accuracy, but, as with any company-issued announcement, this is marketing, not evidence.

The appeal for new customers is mainly price. Entry offers and low per-minute rates make it easy to try a reading without a large outlay. That is helpful if you want to see what a reading feels like, but it is also a reason to be disciplined. Low rates can lead to long sessions, and the total adds up.

We rank Keen fourth, behind Medium Reading, Kasamba and California Psychics. The main reason is the uncertainty over policy details on the live site, combined with variable reader quality typical of large open marketplaces. See the full ranking for the comparison.

How Keen works

Sign-up

You create an account and, according to public descriptions, provide a payment method such as a credit card, PayPal or Apple Pay. This is standard for services that offer a low-cost trial and then bill by the minute. Before you enter details, read what happens at the end of the promotional period.

Selecting an advisor

Advisors are listed by category. Typical categories on this type of platform include love and relationships, tarot, astrology, career, dream interpretation and mediumship. Each profile shows the advisor’s rate, rating, review text and usually an availability indicator.

Starting a session

You connect by the channel the advisor offers. Phone and chat are the most common. As with any per-minute service, your cost depends on the clock, so keep track of elapsed time, and remember that chat includes typing delays.

Minimums and payment

We could not confirm minimum session lengths or the full list of accepted payment methods from Keen’s own pages. Check these at checkout.

Pricing and the intro offer

Several public sources report that new customers can get five minutes for one dollar, with ongoing rates starting at around two dollars per minute. That is among the lowest entry points in the industry, but remember three caveats. First, offers change and vary by campaign. Second, the introductory deal may be limited to certain advisors. Third, after the promotional minutes end, you pay the advisor’s standard rate, which can be much higher than the headline.

A practical example: if you take five minutes for a dollar and then continue for ten more minutes at a rate of four dollars per minute, you will have spent about forty-one dollars. That may be perfectly reasonable for you, but it is not the one dollar the banner suggests. Decide your limit before you begin.

Satisfaction policy and refunds

This is where public reports disagree. One review describes a guarantee of up to twenty-five dollars toward a reading with a different advisor if you are unhappy for any reason. Another source lists a “100 percent satisfaction guarantee” without giving conditions. A third says there is no satisfaction guarantee on promotional readings. These cannot all be true as stated, and the most careful reading is that a credit-style guarantee may exist but may not apply to promotional sessions.

If you care about the guarantee, ask support before you start whether the one-dollar introductory session is covered, and keep their reply. Support hours are also reported differently by different sources, so check the contact page for the current numbers and hours.

If something goes wrong, document the date, time, advisor name and what you were billed. For card disputes, your issuer has its own process. Our guide to getting an accurate psychic reading covers keeping notes.

How to pick a reader on Keen

We do not recommend named readers, since lists change and we cannot verify current listings. Use these steps instead.

  1. Define the question. Choose a category before you browse, so you do not wander through profiles without a purpose.
  2. Sort and filter thoughtfully. Sorting by rating alone can favour accounts with few reviews. Look at the review count together with the score.
  3. Read the recent reviews. Older praise may no longer reflect how an advisor works today. Look for comments that mention specific, checkable details.
  4. Compare at least three advisors. Look at tone, rate and how clearly each describes their method.
  5. Avoid certainty claims. Someone who guarantees outcomes, such as a partner returning on a specific date, is overpromising.
  6. Use the cheap first minutes as a test. Ask one precise question and judge whether the answer is relevant and honest.

A reader who tells you that you are cursed, that money must be paid to lift a problem, or that you must keep talking to avoid harm should be avoided. We list more red flags in Are psychics real?

Privacy and safety

Treat a marketplace as you would any online service. Do not share banking details, government identification numbers, passwords or the private information of other people. Read the privacy policy to learn what the service stores, particularly for chat transcripts. If you use a promotional sign-up, consider whether you want marketing emails and adjust preferences afterward.

The entertainment framing applies. If you are worried about your safety or mental health, a reading is not a replacement for professional support. Be especially cautious with readers who comment on illness, legal matters or investments, because they are not qualified to advise on these subjects.
