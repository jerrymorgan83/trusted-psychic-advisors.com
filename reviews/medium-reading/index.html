What Medium Reading is

Medium Reading presents itself as a place to connect with mediums for evidential readings and spiritual guidance. Its public pages describe it as compassionate, transparent about ratings and available at any hour. The headline on its home page invites visitors to “reconnect with your loved one’s message,” and a badge beneath it reads “Free First Question, No Signup Fee.” Unlike general psychic marketplaces that list tarot, astrology, love and career advisors side by side, Medium Reading is narrow by design. Almost everything on the site relates to communication with people who have died, interpreting signs and symbols, and coping with loss.

That focus is the main reason it ranks first in our comparison. A narrow service tends to produce clearer expectations: you know why you are there, the language is consistent, and there is less pressure to buy unrelated products. The site also hosts a set of explanatory and supportive pages, including an explanation of how mediumship is said to work, a frequently asked questions page, grief resources, a reflection journal with prompts, and a dream symbol dictionary, alongside the usual privacy, cookie, terms and disclaimer pages.

It is worth saying what the site is not. It is not a licensed counselling service, and it says so. Its own disclaimer states that readings are for entertainment and self-reflection only and are not a substitute for therapy or medical advice. We think that candour is a point in its favour.

The focus on mediumship: loved ones, grief and signs

Mediumship is the practice, claimed by some people, of communicating with those who have died. We want to be straightforward about this: there is no scientific evidence that anyone can do it, and nothing in this review should be read as saying otherwise. What we can assess is the quality of the service as an experience: how it frames its offering, how it treats vulnerable customers, how transparent it is, and whether it avoids the manipulative habits common in this industry.

The site describes several kinds of reading:

  • Evidential mediumship: a reading that aims to include specific details the customer can recognise, such as a name, a habit or a shared memory, rather than only general comforting statements.
  • Channelled written reading: a message written in a reflective, letter-like style.
  • Symbol and sign interpretation: help thinking about things people often notice after a loss, such as repeated numbers, songs, feathers, scents or an animal that keeps appearing.
  • Dream-contact reading: a look at vivid dreams in which a loved one appears.
  • Guidance for grief: reflective support around the practical and emotional side of loss.

For people dealing with loss, the value of this kind of focus is largely emotional. Grief often comes with a need to talk about the person, to find meaning in small coincidences and to feel that the relationship has not simply ended. A reading framed around those needs may feel more relevant than a generic fortune-telling menu. At the same time, grief makes people vulnerable, which is exactly why we weigh restraint and honesty so heavily in our scoring.

The free first question: how it works, as the site describes it

The site states that you can submit your first question for free. According to its own pages, there is no sign-up fee, no card is required for that first question, and there is no obligation to continue. The answer is described as written, from a real and vetted medium rather than a script, and the site advertises an average response time of around fifteen minutes. A hero statistic on the page also claims more than 1,500 readings given and an average client rating of 4.9; these are the site’s own figures, which we cannot verify, so treat them as marketing claims rather than independent data.

The process as described is simple:

  1. You visit the site and write your question, along with a little context about who you want to hear from or what you want to understand.
  2. A medium writes a response. The site says this is a real person and never a script.
  3. You read the answer at your own pace. If it resonates and you want more, you can look at a longer paid reading.

Please note that we have not verified how fast replies arrive or how detailed they are in practice. If you try the free question, a sensible test is to check how the answer treats your question. Does it respond to what you actually asked? Does it avoid certainty about future events? Does it stay gentle without being vague to the point of meaninglessness? Does it invite you to continue without pressure? Those are the signals we would look for.

Terms are set by the flow you land in. The site’s promise of a free first question is its own statement. As with any offer, the exact conditions are those shown at the moment you use it, so read what is on screen before you enter any personal or payment details.

Written replies: why format matters

Most online psychic platforms are built around live minutes: you call or chat and the meter runs. Medium Reading’s emphasis on written replies changes the experience in several ways.

  • No clock pressure. You are not paying per minute while you think or become emotional. For a topic like loss, that matters.
  • You can reread. Written answers can be reviewed days later, when the first emotional wave has passed. Many people find that a second reading shows them what felt true and what felt generic.
  • Less performance. A live call can feel like a performance in which you are tempted to fill silences with details, which a skilled reader can reflect back. Writing your question in advance gives you more control over what you disclose.
  • Less immediacy. The trade-off is that you cannot ask a follow-up in the moment. If you want a back-and-forth conversation, a live chat on a platform such as Kasamba or Keen may suit you better.

Who Medium Reading suits

Based on what the site publishes, Medium Reading is a good fit if you:

  • have lost someone and want a gentle, reflective way to think about that relationship;
  • are curious about signs, symbols or dreams and want a structured interpretation;
  • prefer written communication to live calls;
  • want to try something without handing over a card first;
  • dislike high-pressure sales and fear-based messaging.

Who should look elsewhere

It is not the best choice if you:

  • want a live conversation with an advisor in real time;
  • want tarot, astrology or career readings from a broad menu of specialists;
  • need to know the full price of a long session before starting, since the site does not publish prices;
  • are looking for predictions about events, a partner’s behaviour or money, which no reading can provide reliably;
  • are in acute crisis. Please talk to a mental health professional or a crisis service first.

How to ask a good question to a medium

The quality of any reading depends heavily on the question. Here is how to write one that gives you something to work with.

Be specific about who, not about what you expect

Mention the person’s relationship to you and perhaps a first name, then stop. You do not need to explain the cause of death, family history or every detail. Giving fewer details makes it easier to judge whether the reply contains anything that feels specific and unprompted.

Ask open, reflective questions

Questions that begin with “what,” “how” or “what might” invite more useful replies than yes-or-no questions. Examples:

  • “I think often about my mother. Is there a message you feel I should hear about how to honour her?”
  • “What might help me find peace around the things left unsaid with my brother?”
  • “I keep seeing the same bird near the house since my husband died. How might I think about that?”

Avoid questions that ask for certainty or control

Questions such as “Will I see them again by next year?” or “Tell me exactly who is with me now” invite confident claims that cannot be checked. A good medium, by the standards we apply, will usually reframe them gently.

Decide in advance what would count as helpful

Before you submit, write down one sentence about what you hope to feel afterwards: lighter, less alone, more able to talk about the person. After reading the answer, compare. If it helps, good. If it does not, you have lost nothing but time, which is exactly the point of a free first question.

What to expect

A realistic expectation is a reflective, warm piece of writing that addresses your question with a mix of specifics and general comfort. Some people find it moving. Others find it generic. Both reactions are common to this kind of service. Here are some points to keep in mind:

  • It will not prove anything. Even a reply that feels strikingly personal can be explained by the information you shared, by common experiences of loss and by how we naturally read meaning into text. Hold the experience lightly.
  • It may stir emotion. Give yourself time afterwards. Do not make big decisions the same day.
  • It will not diagnose or advise. The site says it is not therapy and not medical or legal advice.
  • Further steps are optional. The site says it avoids fear tactics and guarantees. If at any point you feel pushed, that is a sign to stop.

Privacy and data

The site publishes privacy and cookie pages and says it offers data deletion on request. As with any online service where you may share sensitive personal stories, we recommend a cautious approach:

  • Share only as much as your question needs. You can describe a relationship without sharing full names, addresses or financial details.
  • Read the current privacy policy before entering your email address or other details.
  • Use a dedicated email address if you would prefer to keep readings separate from your main inbox.
  • If you decide to request deletion of your data, keep a record of the request.

We have not audited the site’s data practices. The above describes what the site publishes and common-sense precautions.

Pricing transparency: the honest caveat

This is the main weakness we found. Medium Reading does not publish prices on the pages we reviewed. Its booking page explains that the link opens an independent booking partner and tells you to check that partner’s current prices, formats, availability and terms. We consider this honest, because it does not imply prices it cannot control. But it also means that you cannot see in advance what a longer reading would cost, how long it would take, or what the refund rules are.

How to handle this sensibly:

  1. Use the free first question first. It is the only part the site itself promises at no cost.
  2. If you proceed to a paid reading, read the booking partner’s price, format, delivery time, refund and cancellation terms before you enter payment details.
  3. Check that the checkout page is on a domain you recognise and that the amount shown matches what you expect.
  4. Do not buy extra “remedies” or add-ons. The site says it avoids fear-based tactics; if a later step contradicts that, it is a reason to walk away.

We rate Medium Reading highly in spite of this, but we would score it even higher if the full price structure were visible on the site.

Frequently asked questions about Medium Reading

Is Medium Reading really free?

The site states that your first question is free, with no sign-up fee and no card required. Any further readings are handled through an independent booking partner, whose prices and terms you should check before paying.

How much does a paid reading cost?

The site does not publish prices. Its booking page tells visitors to check the partner’s current prices, formats, availability and terms, so confirm the amount at checkout before you commit.

How fast do I get an answer?

The site advertises an average response time of around fifteen minutes and says some replies take longer. That is the site’s own claim and we have not verified it.

Are the mediums real people?

The site says replies come from real, vetted mediums and never from a script. It also states that the profile portraits and editorial profiles on the site are illustrative and do not identify specific practitioners, so do not rely on a profile as proof of who will write to you.

Can a medium really talk to my loved one?

There is no scientific evidence that mediums can communicate with the dead. Some people find readings comforting as a way to reflect on a relationship and process grief. Approach it with curiosity and keep your expectations realistic.

Is Medium Reading a replacement for therapy?

No. The site says readings are for entertainment and self-reflection only. If grief is overwhelming, persistent or affecting daily life, please contact a licensed counsellor, your doctor or a crisis service.

What should I write in my question?

Name the relationship, add a short piece of context and ask an open question such as what you might hear about honouring the person. Avoid yes-or-no questions and questions that demand certainty about the future.

Is my information private?

The site publishes privacy and cookie pages and says it offers data deletion on request. Share only what your question needs and read the current policy before submitting personal details.

Is there a money-back guarantee?

We found no published money-back guarantee on the site. The site says it avoids guarantees about outcomes. Refund terms for paid readings are set by the booking partner, so read them first.

How does it compare with Kasamba or California Psychics?

Those are large marketplaces billed per minute, with many advisors across many topics. Medium Reading is a focused mediumship service with written replies. See our full comparison and the Kasamba and California Psychics reviews.
