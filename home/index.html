    How to Choose an Advisor

    Three questions to answer before you spend a cent.

    1

    Name the problem before you pick a platform

    Every reading goes better when you arrive with a real question. If you want relationship clarity, name the person and the timeline that worries you. If money is the concern, name the number behind it, the job offer or the debt you're carrying, instead of asking a reader to guess what's bothering you.

    Our catalog groups all 13,166 advisors by specialty, and we've tagged 214 of them for spiritual readings alone, so once you know your category you can filter instead of scrolling a flat list. Vague questions get vague answers on any platform. Bring the decision, not the mood, and the reading has something to push against.

    2

    Read the specific reviews behind the star average

    A 4.8 average tells you almost nothing about whether a reader fits your situation. Open the reviews left by clients who booked a session like yours, a breakup reading or a career call, and read what they said the advisor got right.

    Psychic Source carries more than 270,000 five-star reviews across its roster, enough volume that you can find someone who described your exact situation. On smaller platforms, read the most recent reviews for the specific reader, not the platform-wide score, before you book.

    3

    Test the connection before you pay for a long session

    Every platform on this list lets you start small. Kasamba gives new members three free minutes and Keen sells five minutes for a dollar. Purple Garden opens with a $10 credit instead. Use that window to judge tone and pace before you book a longer call.

    A short trial call also tells you whether a reader talks in specifics or in comfortable generalities. If the first few minutes are all reassurance and no detail, a longer session is unlikely to change that. Trust the fit you feel on the cheap call before you spend real money on the expensive one.

    What Do You Actually Want to Ask?

    The right question decides whether a reading is useful or a waste of money.

    Love and relationships

    A relationship reading works best when you bring a specific person and a specific fork in the road, not a general wish to feel better about your love life. Give a reader a real decision to work with. A session built around "make me feel better" tends to run long and land nowhere.

    • Is this relationship moving toward commitment or stalling?
    • What pattern keeps repeating with the people I date?
    • Should I reach out to them again, or wait?
    • Will I find love?
    • Am I going to be happy?

    Career and money

    Career and money questions get sharper answers when you name the actual decision on the table, the offer or the resignation you're weighing, instead of asking whether you'll ever be successful. Bring a number or a deadline, and the reader has an anchor. Ask about your future in general terms and you'll get an answer in general terms back.

    • Should I take the new job offer or stay put?
    • Is this business partnership worth the risk?
    • When is the right time to ask for a raise?
    • Will I ever be rich?
    • Is money coming my way soon?

    Life direction and loss

    Grief and direction readings ask a lot of a stranger on the phone, so give them something concrete to work with, a name and the decision you keep circling back to. Say who you lost or what fork in the road is keeping you up. A reader can meet you there. A vague ache about "what's next" leaves them guessing along with you.

    • What would my father want me to know right now?
    • Am I on the right path with this move?
    • How do I make peace with a decision I already made?
    • What is my purpose?
    • Is everything going to be okay?

    Reading Types Explained

    Reading Format

    Tarot

    Tarot works through a deck of cards, each carrying its own meaning that shifts with its position in the spread and the cards drawn beside it. A reader lays the cards out and reads the pattern across the spread rather than any single card by itself. The images give both of you something to point at, which helps when you can't put the question into words yet.

    You don't need to believe the cards fix your future for a tarot session to help. Treat the spread as a mirror for a decision you're already leaning toward, and the reading works as a structured conversation more than a fortune. It suits you if you prefer a visual, tactile session over a straight verbal reading. Ask the reader to explain why a specific card landed where it did, since a session that skips that step hands you a verdict instead of a conversation.

    • Weighing a decision you keep circling
    • Sorting mixed feelings about one relationship
    • A visual session instead of pure talk
    • Ongoing check-ins on a single question

    Reading Format

    Astrology

    Astrology reads your birth chart, the position of the planets at the exact time and place you were born, and maps those placements onto your life, from relationships to career timing. A reading walks through your chart and connects specific placements to patterns you already recognize in yourself.

    A good astrology reader spends the session on your actual chart, not a generic sun-sign horoscope you could read in a magazine. It suits you if you want a reading grounded in a repeatable method, since two astrologers working from your birth data should land on similar core themes. Bring your exact birth time if you have it. A rough guess can shift an entire house and throw the whole reading off.

    • Long-range personality and timing questions
    • Compatibility between you and one partner
    • Career timing tied to your chart
    • A repeatable method over pure intuition
    Find astrology readers →
    Reading Format

    Numerology

    Numerology reduces your birth date and your full name to a small set of core numbers, then reads meaning into each one. A numerologist often starts with your life-path number and builds outward from there, connecting the digits to decisions you're facing now.

    The method rewards you if you like a session built on calculation rather than symbols or planetary charts. It works well as a short, focused reading: your birth data fixes the numbers, so the conversation moves fast once the reader has your details. This one tends to run shorter than the other four types here, since the reader spends less time interpreting and more time calculating.

    • A quick session with fixed inputs
    • Naming a theme for the year ahead
    • Compatibility read through shared numbers
    • Good for a first, low-cost reading

    Reading Format

    Mediumship

    A mediumship reading centers on a specific person who has died, and the session opens with the reader asking for a first name or an object connected to them. The reader's own method decides what happens next, so this is one category where the specific advisor matters more than the platform.

    Go in with a name and a memory in mind, not a general wish to hear from someone. The strongest sessions we've sat through connected to a detail the reader had no obvious way to know in advance, which is the bar worth holding a mediumship reading to. Not every attempt connects on the first try, and a reader worth booking again will say when nothing came through instead of stretching a vague impression into a story.

    • Connecting to one named person
    • Processing a recent, specific loss
    • Sessions where advisor choice matters most
    • Closure around unfinished conversations

    Reading Format

    Palmistry and dreams

    Palmistry reads the lines and shape of your hand, often over video so the reader can see your palm, while dream readings work from a dream you describe in your own words. Both methods start from something concrete you bring to the call instead of a birth chart or a name.

    These sessions suit you if you want a lighter entry point into a reading, something to try over a video chat without booking a long phone call about a major life decision. They also work well paired with a second reading type once a theme surfaces. A palm reading pairs well with astrology, and a recurring dream often points a reader toward a question worth taking to tarot next.

    • A low-stakes first reading to try
    • Best done over video, not phone
    • Working through a recurring dream
    • Pairs well with a follow-up reading

    Our Editorial Standards

    The rules our own reviews have to follow, in plain language.

    How we test

    Every review starts with a real booking. An editor opens an account on the platform and pays with a personal card, then sits through the full session, whether that means a short chat or a longer phone call. We log the wait time before a reader picks up and the price once the meter stops, then check the reading against the question we asked going in. We run this process across all six platforms inside the same testing window, so a platform's score reflects its current service instead of a stale one. If a reader disappoints us on one call, we book a second session with a different advisor before we adjust the score. One bad match shouldn't sink an entire company's rating.

    How we stay independent

    None of the six platforms on this list can buy a higher score. We accept referral fees when a reader clicks through and signs up on a platform we recommend, the way most review sites fund themselves, but we fix that fee regardless of rank and never tie it to which platform we place first. Our editors sign every review with their own name, so a piece carries a byline someone has to stand behind. If a platform's terms or pricing change in a way that affects the score, we revise the entry that month rather than waiting for the next refresh. The catalog under our advisor catalog follows the same rule: an advisor's presence there means we verified the listing, not that we endorse the individual reader.

    Scoring Method

    How We Score a Platform

    1

    Book the same way you would

    An editor creates an account on the platform using a personal email and pays with a personal card, the same signup path open to anyone reading this page. We skip press access and platform-provided demo accounts, since a demo session tells you nothing about the wait or the price a paying caller faces.

    2

    Time the wait and log the cost

    We record the minutes between requesting a reader and the session starting, along with the total charge once the call ends, including any per-minute overage past a package's included time.

    3

    Score the reading against the question asked

    Before the call, we write down the specific question we plan to ask. After, we compare the reader's answer against that question and note whether the advice holds up as a plan you could act on.

    4

    Average across a second session per platform

    We repeat the test with a different advisor on the same platform, since one reader's bad night shouldn't set a company's score. The final number averages both sessions, and if the two scores land far apart, an editor books a third to break the tie.

    No platform pays a fee to appear in this ranking or to move up within it. A platform improves its position only by scoring higher on the next monthly test.
